Aerospace tooling

Invar tooling plate for dimensional stability.

Aerospace tooling is not an LNG-strip problem at larger thickness. Plate route, residual stress, thermal history, weld behavior and machining stability become the decisive variables.

Application-specific sourcing
Tooling requirements

Low CTE is necessary, but not sufficient.

Large tooling plates and welded structures must remain dimensionally stable through machining, assembly and thermal cycles.

01

CTE curve

Confirm the relevant temperature range rather than a single nominal expansion value.

02

Residual stress

Stress-relief route and machining sequence affect distortion risk.

03

Plate flatness

Incoming flatness and thickness variation influence final tool geometry.

04

UT quality

Ultrasonic testing and internal soundness are reviewed for thick plate or forged plate routes.

05

Thermal cycling

Trial qualification should consider the actual composite cure cycle or service temperature.

06

Weld stability

Weld procedure and post-weld distortion control are part of the engineering route.

Commercial route

Separate tooling qualification from generic Invar availability.

The right source is selected against plate dimensions, low-stress condition, inspection and machining requirements.

01

Plate / forged plate

Product form is chosen by thickness, width, internal quality and residual-stress target.

02

Machining allowance

Supply dimensions must include realistic stock for finish machining.

03

Trial evidence

CTE data, UT, heat treatment and dimensional behavior are reviewed before scale-up.

04

Project support

Non-standard dimensions can be evaluated through mill and processor development routes.
